A

Sticker Sheet

The smallest change that fixes both complaints. Filters become paper stickers, each already showing its run count and accuracy, selected with a highlighter swipe. The line keeps its shape — the same numbers, the same reading order — but every run becomes a star sized by the score, on a violet wash, with the personal best crowned and annotated in pencil.

    Why it works

    Nothing to read before you can act: the count and the accuracy bar are on the card, so the click confirms rather than reveals. Activities she has not tried are dashed and invite rather than filter to nothing.

    The chart

    Star size encodes the score twice — position and area — so a good run is visibly bigger, not just higher. The gold record star and the dashed "56 to beat" give the next game a target.

    Cost to build

    Small. practice_filter_form() gains a counts query; practice_line_svg() gains a star path and a max marker. The pinned geometry survives — same viewBox, same x/y maths.

    B

    Night Sky

    The chart stops being a chart and becomes a piece of sky taped into the workbook. Each run is a star whose brightness is its accuracy and whose height is its score; a trail joins them in order; the personal best throws a shooting star. Score belts across the sky give the numbers meaning without an axis. The activity cards borrow the same night palette so the two halves of the page read as one idea.

      Why it works

      One inverted panel on a page of squared paper is the most memorable thing on the site — the page gets an identity, not just a coat of paint. The belts turn a raw score into a rank she can aim at.

      The risk

      It is the boldest of the three and the least like the rest of the tracker. If the maths board inherits it too, the whole service changes character; if it does not, Spanish looks special on purpose.

      Cost to build

      Medium. New SVG renderer with a gradient, a glow filter and a deterministic star field, plus a dark skin for the cards. The golden snapshot needs re-pinning for the Spanish chart.

      C

      Star Ladder

      Drop the line entirely. Each game is a column drawn straight onto the grid squares, and three star tiers cut across it — one star at 20, two at 35, three at 50. A run is no longer a dot on a trend, it is a score that did or did not earn its third star, and the whole chart answers "how do I get better" without anyone explaining the y-axis. The cards carry the same tier ring.

        Why it works

        Stars are earned, not decorative. Because the tiers are fixed, a short 17-question game and a 69-question one are honestly not the same achievement — and the chart says so.

        Watch out

        Fixed tiers reward long games. If she plays a quick five-minute set, the third star is out of reach through no fault of her own — the tiers may need to scale with questions attempted, or sit on accuracy instead.

        Cost to build

        Medium. A new bars panel type beside line, with the tier thresholds as panel configuration so maths can set its own. No change to the storage model.

        Recommendation: A's filter cards with C's chart. The sticker cards are the right answer for the controls in any of the three — they are cheap, they show the answer before the click, and they degrade gracefully as more activities appear. The star ladder is the right answer for the chart, because it is the only one of the three that gives her something to aim at rather than something to look at.

        Take B if the goal is that she opens the page for its own sake. Its shooting star is the only element here that rewards a personal best in the moment it happens.
