The weekly timetable, three ways

Same data, same status vocabulary, same single judge_week() call — three layouts for the section that sits above the subjects list on /. Pick one to run first; all three ship behind meta.timetable_design so a week with the student decides it, not this page.

Her questionWhat am I in now, what is next, what have I done today.
His questionOn Friday: which blocks this week have nothing logged against them.
The ruleStudy blocks are never self-ticked. A block is done when a session, attempt or practice run exists for that subject on that date. Derived, so honest.
NeverNo streaks, no "days in a row". A missed block is a red cross, not a hidden one — and only Dad can excuse it, with the reason shown.

Design A was chosen. This deck is the record of that decision, kept for the reasoning. B and C were not built, and are not in the service — the week strip is the only board there is.

A

Week strip

Five columns, Monday to Friday, each a stack of thin block chips. Today's column carries a red-pencil TODAY tab and lifts off the paper; the rest lie flat. On a phone it becomes a snap-scrolling strip that opens on today. One monospace line underneath carries the week's totals.

education.rmmann.co.uk/?design=a
380 px
Why

The whole-week glance is the parent's actual need, and it is here without a click. A vertical run of marks makes a bad day jump out as a shape before you read a word. It also matches the "sessions by week" model the rest of the tracker already uses.

Weakness

The current block is only as loud as its chip — the student's first question gets the same 12 px as everything else. Mitigated with the pulsing ring and the phone strip opening on today, but B beats it outright on this.

Cost to build

Small. One grid, one chip partial, one totals line. No new state, no JS required — the phone auto-scroll is a four-line enhancement.

B

Now / next, then the week

One big card for the block she is in, with a time bar that visibly drains (pure CSS, a negative animation-delay computed server-side — no JavaScript). Under it the next block in one line, then today as a checklist that fills from the top, then a compact dot-row for the week that expands a day inline.

education.rmmann.co.uk/?design=b
380 px
Why

Closest to the research the timetable came from: time made visible rather than merely stated, one thing at a time, and endowed progress — the movement block ticks first thing and the checklist is already started before the hard block begins.

Weakness

The parent's whole-week view shrinks to a dot-row. Fix by making /week/{iso} the expanded board and linking it from the dot-row, so Friday's review is one tap away rather than on the page.

Cost to build

Medium. Three components instead of one, plus the drain animation's start/end arithmetic. All of it still server-rendered.

C

Register

The school register. Rows are time slots, columns are days, today's column shaded paper-yellow under a red-pencil header, the current cell in a pencil box. A footer row counts each day. Prints on one sheet.

education.rmmann.co.uk/?design=c
380 px · collapsed to today
Why

Densest of the three and instantly familiar to a UK parent. Misses read as patterns rather than incidents — a row of crosses at 13:00 says the timed handwritten block is being avoided, which no other layout shows.

Weakness

Least mobile-friendly by some distance, and the week's real shape fights it: Wednesday stops at 10:00 and Thursday at 12:00, so the bottom third of the table is mostly blank paper. On a phone it has to collapse to one column.

Cost to build

Medium. The slot union and the mobile collapse are the work; the print stylesheet is nearly free once the table exists.

Recommendation: run B for her, and make /week/{iso} design A for him. The two readers want genuinely different things and the split is cheap, because all three already share one judge_week() and one status vocabulary — the difference is markup.

B wins the daily case on the research alone: the draining time bar is the one element here that addresses time blindness instead of assuming it away, and the checklist that fills from the top is endowed progress working as intended. A wins Friday: the parent's question is comparative across five days, and a grid of marks answers it without a tap.

C is worth keeping behind the switch for a term. If the same slot keeps coming up crossed, it will be C that shows it — but that is a monthly question, not a daily one, so it belongs on a printed sheet in the kitchen rather than on /.

Nothing is decided here. All three go in behind meta.timetable_design, and the switch stays until you have run each for a week or two with her.
