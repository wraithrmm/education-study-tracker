# Teaching AQA English Language 8700 — the method

Read when the session is Language. Both papers are 1h45, 80 marks, 50% each; **160 raw marks**
total; untiered; handwritten. Reading 50% / Writing 50% in each paper.

**Assessment changes from summer 2026 onwards (apply to her June 2027 sitting):** Paper 1 Q1 is
multiple choice; Q3 focuses on a single structural effect; Q5's narrative option asks for the
*opening* of a story and the description option treats the image as inspiration only. Mark
allocations, question count, timings and AOs are unchanged. Papers from 2017–2025 remain valid
practice for Q2, Q4 and both writing tasks; note the Q1/Q3 wording difference when using them.

## Paper 1 — Explorations in creative reading and writing (fiction source, 20th/21st c.)

| Q | Marks | AO | Time | Shape she learns |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 4 | AO1 | 5 min | Multiple choice from the given lines. Read the lines *first*, then the options. |
| 2 | 8 | AO2 language | 10 min | Two or three points on how the writer uses **language** in the given lines: word/phrase → method → effect. Write about the *words*, not the plot. |
| 3 | 8 | AO2 structure | 10 min | **One structural effect**, tracked across the whole source: what the reader sees first / what shifts / where the focus narrows or widens, and why. |
| 4 | 20 | AO4 evaluate | 20 min | A statement to evaluate. **Agree with reasons**: three points, each with evidence, method, and "this works because…". The biggest reading block — never blank. |
| 5 | 40 (24 AO5 + 16 AO6) | writing | 45 min | Description (image as inspiration) **or** story opening. Plan 5 / write 35 / check 5. |

Reading the source: 10–15 minutes at the start is built into the timing. Teach her to read the
questions first, then the source, annotating for Q2 lines and Q4's statement.

## Paper 2 — Writers' viewpoints and perspectives (two non-fiction sources, one 19th c.)

| Q | Marks | AO | Time | Shape |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 4 | AO1 | 5 min | Four true statements from a list of eight. Check every statement against the lines. |
| 2 | 8 | AO1 summarise/synthesise | 10 min | Summary of the **differences** (or similarities) between the two sources on a given focus: point → evidence from A → evidence from B → inference. No analysis of language. **Both sources or Level 3 is unreachable.** |
| 3 | 12 | AO2 language | 12 min | How the writer of one source uses language to [effect]. Same as P1 Q2 but longer. |
| 4 | 16 | AO3 compare | 18 min | Compare the **writers' viewpoints/attitudes** and how they convey them. Perspective first, then methods, both sources in every paragraph. **Both sources or Level 3 is unreachable.** |
| 5 | 40 (24 AO5 + 16 AO6) | writing | 45 min | Transactional: letter / article / speech / leaflet / essay, arguing a point of view on the sources' theme. |

The 19th-century source is the one that scares students. Teach period vocabulary and long
sentence habits explicitly, and note that Literature's 19th-century novel is doing the same job.

## Writing — the half she is good at

### Q5 Paper 1: descriptive or narrative opening (her banker)

Descriptive is the default choice unless the image gives her nothing. The mark scheme's top band
(AO5 Level 4 upper, 22–24) describes writing that is **convincing and compelling, with extensive
and ambitious vocabulary, sustained crafting of linguistic devices, varied and inventive
structural features, and fluently linked paragraphs**. In her terms:

- **Answer the task set.** A description or opening that drifts off the stated focus has its
  AO5 mark capped at 12/24, whatever the quality. The focus word goes in the plan.
- **One setting, one mood, one time of day.** Pick a mood word before anything else (eerie,
  serene, chaotic). Every image serves it. Sprawl is the top-band killer.
- **Structure is a choice she makes, not an accident.** Zoom in → zoom out; a shift (weather
  changes, someone arrives); a cyclical ending that echoes the opening; a "camera" that moves
  through the scene. Name the structure in the plan.
- **Sensory detail chosen, not listed.** Two senses beyond sight per paragraph, and the unexpected
  one wins (the *taste* of a place).
- **Vocabulary she owns.** Ambitious but correct beats ambitious and misspelt.
- **Punctuation she owns.** Semicolons, colons, dashes earn AO6 marks only when right. Drill one
  at a time until it's automatic, then licence it.
- **Sentence variety on purpose.** A one-word sentence after a long one. Read the paragraph aloud
  to hear it.
- **Narrative opening:** hook, one character, tension, stop before the story explains itself.
  Since 2026 she is not writing a whole story — no rushed endings.

Plan format (5 min, on the page): mood word · structure name · four things in the scene · two
sensory details each · the last line.

### Q5 Paper 2: transactional

Plan: **form, audience, purpose** written at the top; a one-line thesis; three arguments, each
with an example; the opposing view acknowledged and answered; a closing call. Register matches
the form (a speech addresses the room; a letter has a salutation and sign-off; an article has a
headline). Persuasive devices appear because they persuade this audience — an examiner marks
"used a rhetorical question" as nothing; "used a rhetorical question that makes the reader
implicate themselves" as craft.

### AO6 — technical accuracy (16 marks per paper, 20% of the GCSE)

One AO6 focus per session, applied in her own writing that day, not in an exercise:
comma splices → full stops or semicolons; apostrophes; homophones; paragraphing (a new paragraph
at every shift of time, place, person, idea); sentence-type variety; spelling of ambitious words
she keeps using. The Level 4 AO6 descriptor asks for sentence demarcation that is **consistently
secure and accurate**, a wide range of punctuation **used with a high level of accuracy**, and
**a full range of sentence forms for effect**.

## Retrieval starters (Language)

Quick, ~5 min: "What's Q3 asking, in one sentence?" · "Give me three structural moves for a
description." · "Fix this comma splice." · "What are the four things in a P2 Q5 plan?" · a
two-minute Q2 on four lines of any text.

## Common examiner-report complaints to design against

- Q2/Q3: technique-spotting; word-level analysis with no reference to what the words describe.
- Q3: writing about language instead of structure; not tracking across the whole source.
- Q4: describing rather than evaluating; ignoring the statement given.
- P2 Q2: analysing language in a summary question.
- P2 Q4: comparing content instead of viewpoints; one source dominating.
- Q5: unplanned writing; too many ideas; shifting mood; ambitious punctuation used wrongly;
  narrative option written as a full story with a rushed ending; a picture "described" item by item.
- Running out of time for Q5 because reading overran — or the reverse: skipping Q4.
