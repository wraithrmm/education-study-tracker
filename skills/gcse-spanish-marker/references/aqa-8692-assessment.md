# AQA GCSE Spanish 8692 — assessment structure and criteria

Reproduced from the published specification (v1.1, 16 Sep 2025) so marking needs no lookup for the
structure or the criteria. **The series mark scheme still wins** for accepted answers, translation
element lists and worked examples. First exams June 2026.

Source: `https://www.aqa.org.uk/subjects/spanish/gcse/spanish-8692/specification/scheme-of-assessment`

## 1. Structure

Tiered: **Foundation grades 1–5, Higher grades 4–9.** All four components at the same tier, same
series. Three themes: People and lifestyle · Popular culture · Communication and the world around
us. No dictionaries in any component.

| Paper | Foundation | Higher | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Listening | 40 marks, ~35 min | 50 marks, ~45 min | 25% |
| 2 Speaking (NEA) | 50 marks, 7–9 min | 50 marks, 10–12 min | 25% |
| 3 Reading | 50 marks, 45 min | 50 marks, 1 hr | 25% |
| 4 Writing | 50 marks, 1 hr 10 | 50 marks, 1 hr 15 | 25% |

### Component scaling — apply before any grade conversion

| Component | F raw | F factor | H raw | H factor | Scaled max |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Listening | 40 | **×1.25** | 50 | ×1 | 50 |
| Speaking | 50 | ×1 | 50 | ×1 | 50 |
| Reading | 50 | ×1 | 50 | ×1 | 50 |
| Writing | 50 | ×1 | 50 | ×1 | 50 |
| **Total scaled** | | | | | **200** |

Boundaries are set on the total scaled mark of 200 at both tiers.

### Assessment objectives

- **AO1** understand and respond to spoken language (35%)
- **AO2** understand and respond to written language (45%)
- **AO3** knowledge and accurate application of prescribed grammar and vocabulary (20%)

## 2. Paper 1 Listening

- **Section A** comprehension, questions and answers in English or non-verbal. 32 marks F / 40 H.
  Points-marked against the series scheme; the mark is awarded where understanding is
  satisfactorily communicated. Each item heard twice.
- **Section B** dictation. 8 marks F / 10 H. Sentences heard three times. Minimum 20 words F /
  30 H, including two words from outside the vocabulary list. **Band-marked, judged across all
  extracts as a whole**, split AO1 (communication of meaning) and AO3 (transcription and
  grammatical accuracy), 4+4 at F, 5+5 at H.

Dictation AO1 descriptors — meaning of the extracts is: always/almost always communicated clearly
(top) · mostly · sometimes · rarely · below standard (0). Higher adds a top band for "communicated
very clearly throughout".

Dictation AO3 descriptors — words transcribed correctly with grammatical accuracy: frequently/good
(top at F) · generally/reasonable · occasionally/limited · very rarely/very limited · below
standard. Higher adds "always or nearly always / very high" at the top.

**Zero on AO1 forces zero on AO3. Otherwise AO1 does not cap AO3.** This linkage recurs everywhere
in this spec.

## 3. Paper 2 Speaking (NEA)

Conducted and audio-recorded by the teacher in a five-week window in April–May, marked by an AQA
examiner. 15 minutes' supervised preparation. Marking stops at 9 min (F) / 12 min (H).

| Part | Marks | Objectives |
|---|---|---|
| Role-play | 10 | AO2 |
| Reading aloud + 4 compulsory questions | 15 | AO3 5 (pronunciation) + AO1 10 |
| Photo card: description + unprepared conversation | 25 | AO2 5 + AO1 15 + AO3 5 |

**Role-play**, five tasks × 2 marks (AO2): 2 = message conveyed without ambiguity · 1 = partially
conveyed or conveyed with some ambiguity · 0 = no part conveyed. Two required details, one failed
→ 1. Once a task is accomplished, further incorrect information is ignored.

**Reading aloud, pronunciation (5, AO3):** graded by density of minor and major errors. A *major*
error adversely affects communication; a *minor* one does not. Higher's top band is "always or
nearly always accurate, occasional minor error"; Foundation's top band is "minor errors and a few
major errors". **Not markable from a transcript.**

**Response to four compulsory questions (10, AO1),** marked as a whole, not per question:
9–10 all answered clearly, ≥2 extended and ≥1 other developed well · 7–8 ≥3 clear, 1 extended ·
5–6 ≥2 clear, 1 developed well · 3–4 ≥2 understandable, 1 minimally developed · 1–2 ≥1
understandable, possibly very limited · 0 below standard.

**Photo card description (5, AO2):** graded by how much information is conveyed and how clearly.
Higher's bands are stricter at every level. Student must say at least one thing about each photo;
**speaking about only one photo deducts one mark**, except where the mark would have been 1.

**Unprepared conversation, AO1 (15):** bands of three marks, turning on quantity of information,
development of responses (minimal / good / extended) and clarity. **AO3 (5):** variety of
vocabulary and structures, repetition, and the density of minor and major errors — Higher's top
band expects wide variety with few or no major errors when complex language is attempted.

## 4. Paper 3 Reading

- **Section A** comprehension, 40 marks, answers in English or non-verbal. Points-marked against
  the series scheme. Includes inferring plausible meanings of single unknown words from context.
- **Section B** translation Spanish → English, 10 marks. Minimum 35 words F / 50 H. Two-grid
  structure as in §5.

## 5. Paper 4 Writing

### Foundation (50)

| Q | Marks | Objectives | Task |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 10 | AO2 | Five short sentences about a photo, each marked 0–2 |
| 2 | 10 | AO2 5 + AO3 5 | Five compulsory bullets, ~50 words |
| 3 | 5 | AO3 | Five gap sentences, correct word from three options |
| 4 | 10 | AO3 | Translation into Spanish, min 35 words |
| 5 | 15 | AO2 10 + AO3 5 | Three compulsory bullets, ~90 words (overlaps H Q2) |

Q1 per sentence: 2 = relevant message clearly communicated · 1 = relevant but some ambiguity,
delays communication · 0 = irrelevant or not understandable.

Q2 AO2 (5): all five bullets covered and clear (5) down to one bullet with many lapses (1).
Q2 AO3 (5): variety of vocabulary and structures against error density, minor vs major.

### Higher (50)

| Q | Marks | Objectives | Task |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 10 | AO3 | Translation into Spanish, min 50 words |
| 2 | 15 | AO2 10 + AO3 5 | Three compulsory bullets, ~90 words (overlaps F Q5) |
| 3 | 25 | AO2 15 + AO3 10 | Open-ended, two bullets, ~150 words |

**Q3 AO2 (15)**, bands of three: quantity of information relative to the task, successful
development of ideas/accounts/description, and clarity. **A response covering only one bullet
point caps AO2 at 12.**

**Q3 AO3 splits into two grids of 5:**
- *Range and use of language* — variety of appropriate vocabulary and structures, and how often
  complex language is attempted and succeeds.
- *Accuracy* — overall accuracy, the balance of major and minor errors, and explicitly whether
  **verb and tense formations are secure**.

### The 90-word and 150-word AO3 grids (F Q5 / H Q2 and H Q3 range)

These turn on three things together: variety of vocabulary, attempts at complexity, and
**successful reference to time frames** — three time frames mainly successful at the top band, two
at the next, none at the bottom two. Word counts are approximate; the descriptors govern, and all
work is marked even well beyond the suggested length.

## 6. Translation grids (both directions, F Q4 / H Q1 / Reading Section B)

**Grid one — rendering of the original meaning.** The series mark scheme divides the passage into
**15 elements**. Tick each element communicated *despite minor inaccuracies*.

| Ticks | Mark |
|---|---|
| 13–15 | 5 |
| 10–12 | 4 |
| 7–9 | 3 |
| 4–6 | 2 |
| 1–3 | 1 |
| 0 | 0 |

**Grid two — knowledge of vocabulary and grammar**, one judgement out of 5 across the whole
response: 5 very good vocabulary with only minor errors · 4 good, generally accurate, regular
minor errors · 3 satisfactory, more accurate than inaccurate, regular major and minor errors ·
2 limited, generally inaccurate · 1 very limited, highly inaccurate.

**Zero on Grid one forces zero on Grid two.**

## 7. Rules that apply across the whole specification

- **Major error** = adversely affects communication. **Minor error** = does not. Nearly every AO3
  grid turns on this distinction, so classify each error explicitly.
- **Zero on the content objective (AO1 or AO2) forces zero on AO3.** Otherwise the content mark
  never caps the language mark.
- **Language beyond the prescribed vocabulary and grammar gets equal credit** where it fulfils the
  task. Never penalise ambition for being off-list.
- Students must not be disadvantaged by how they refer to gender identity; preferred pronouns,
  gendered language and agreements are credited regardless of official language-body usage.
- Descriptors govern, not word counts.
