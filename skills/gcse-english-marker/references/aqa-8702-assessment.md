Qualification: **160 raw marks**, no scaling — Paper 1's 64 and Paper 2's 96 already embody the
40/60 weighting. Untiered. Closed book. AO weightings overall: AO1 37.5 · AO2 42.5 · AO3 15 · AO4 5.

Assessment objectives (Ofqual, identical across boards):
- **AO1** Read, understand and respond; maintain a critical style; informed personal response;
  textual references including quotations.
- **AO2** Analyse language, form and structure; relevant subject terminology where appropriate.
- **AO3** Relationships between texts and contexts.
- **AO4** Vocabulary and sentence structures for clarity, purpose and effect; accurate spelling
  and punctuation.

## The 30-mark ladder (all set-text questions; unseen Q1 is the same ladder without AO3, scaled to 24)

| Level | Marks (/30) | Marks (/24 unseen) | Characterised by |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6 | 26–30 | 21–24 | **Convincing, critical analysis and exploration.** Critical, exploratory, conceptualised response to task and whole text; judicious precise references; fine-grained, insightful analysis of methods with judicious terminology; convincing exploration of ideas/contexts. |
| 5 | 21–25 | 17–20 | **Thoughtful, developed consideration.** Thoughtful, developed response; apt references integrated into interpretation; examination of methods with effects considered; thoughtful consideration of ideas/contexts. |
| 4 | 16–20 | 13–16 | **Clear understanding.** Clear, explained response; effective use of references to support explanation; clear explanation of methods with effects; clear understanding of ideas/contexts. |
| 3 | 11–15 | 9–12 | **Explained, structured comments.** Some explained response; references used to support a range of relevant comments; identification of methods with some comment on effect; some understanding of ideas/contexts. |
| 2 | 6–10 | 5–8 | **Supported, relevant comments.** Supported response; comments on references; identification of methods (no/limited effect); some awareness of ideas/contexts. |
| 1 | 1–5 | 1–4 | **Simple, explicit comments.** Simple comments relevant to task; reference to relevant details; awareness of the writer making choices; possible reference to context. |

"How to arrive at a mark": at the *top* of a level the response shows the level's features
securely and is starting to show the level above; at the *bottom* it has the level below and is
starting to show elements of this level. Examiners read the whole response and choose the level
of best fit before placing the mark.

**Unseen Q2 (8 marks, AO2 only) — four levels, verbatim descriptors:**
- **Level 4 (7–8):** critical, insightful comparison of poems; analytical comparison of the
  effects of writers' methods to create meanings.
- **Level 3 (5–6):** thoughtful comparison; clear comparison of the effects of methods.
- **Level 2 (3–4):** clear comparison of poems; comments on similarities/differences between the
  effects of methods.
- **Level 1 (1–2):** simple comment on one or both poems and/or simple connection; identification
  of method(s).
- **0:** nothing worthy of credit.

**AO4 (4 marks, Shakespeare & modern text only) — three bands, verbatim from the scheme:**
- **4 marks — high performance:** spell and punctuate with consistent accuracy; consistently use
  vocabulary and sentence structures to achieve effective control of meaning.
- **2–3 marks — intermediate:** considerable accuracy; considerable range of vocabulary and
  sentence structures achieving general control of meaning.
- **1 mark — threshold:** reasonable accuracy and range; any errors do not hinder meaning.
- **0:** nothing written, or below threshold.
Judged "in the context of the level of demand of the question".

## How AQA tells examiners to mark (apply this exactly)

- **Climb from the bottom:** start at Level 1 and go up until the answer no longer meets the
  level. Whole-response judgement; don't pick holes in small parts.
- **Best fit:** good performance in some aspects compensates for shortcomings in others.
  Predominantly Level 3 with a little Level 4 = Level 3, mark near the top. Meeting every
  descriptor in a level = top of that level.
- **Indicative content is a guide.** Credit other valid points; a response need not cover all
  listed points to reach Level 6. Examiners "may well see responses that exceed the Level 6
  descriptors" — use the full range.
- **Rubric infringement cap:** where a response does not address a *defining feature* of the
  task — Paper 1 A and B: extract **and** whole text; Paper 2 B: the named poem **and** one other
  from the cluster — it is capped at the **top of Level 2 (10 marks)**. Apply it, and say so.
- **Telling Tales (Q15–16):** two stories are required; "whole text" means both stories.
  Comparative skills are *not* assessed and must not be rewarded there.
- **AO3 is flexible:** context of writing, context of setting (place, social structures, period),
  literary context (genre), and how different audiences receive the text. Universality counts as
  context. A question that asks for the text "in its context" has AO3 built into the task.
- **Zero** for nothing of relevance to the question.

## Set texts for June 2027 (verified 5 Sep 2026 against the live specification)

- **Shakespeare (6):** Macbeth · Romeo and Juliet · The Tempest · The Merchant of Venice ·
  Much Ado About Nothing · Julius Caesar.
- **19th-century novel (7):** Jekyll and Hyde · A Christmas Carol · Great Expectations · Jane Eyre ·
  Frankenstein · Pride and Prejudice · The Sign of Four.
- **Modern texts (12 live):** An Inspector Calls · Blood Brothers · DNA · A Taste of Honey ·
  Princess & The Hustler · Leave Taking · Lord of the Flies · Telling Tales · Animal Farm ·
  Anita and Me · Pigeon English · My Name is Leon. (The History Boys, Curious Incident and
  Never Let Me Go ended 2024 — do not accept them.)
- **Anthology *Poems Past and Present*, one cluster of 15:** Love and Relationships · Power and
  Conflict · Worlds and Lives.

The student's chosen texts are recorded on the tracker subject row (`notes`). Mark only against
those; a response on a different text is flagged, not marked.

## Recurring examiner-report messages to mark against

- Feature-spotting without effect caps at Level 2–3.
- Bolted-on context is ignored; context integrated into a sentence about the text is rewarded.
- Narrative retelling is Level 1–2 however accurate.
- Close reference in the student's own words earns AO1 like a quotation; inaccurate quotation in
  quotation marks weakens AO1.
- The writer must be the agent ("Shakespeare makes Macbeth…"), not the character.
- Answers must address the question set; excellent irrelevant material earns nothing.
