# AQA GCSE English Language 8700 — assessment reference

Specification: <https://www.aqa.org.uk/subjects/english/gcse/english-8700/specification>
Assessment resources: <https://www.aqa.org.uk/subjects/english/gcse/english-8700/assessment-resources>
Ladders and rules verified against the 8700/1 Sample set 1 mark scheme v1.1 (2026 format) and the
8700/2 June 2023 mark scheme (5 Sep 2026).

## Structure

Two papers, each **1h45, 80 marks, 50%**; **160 raw marks**, no scaling; untiered; handwritten.
Reading 40 / Writing 40 on each paper. Plus the **Spoken Language Endorsement** — a separately
reported Pass/Merit/Distinction/Not Classified that does not affect the 9–1 grade.

| Paper | Q | Marks | AO | Task |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** Explorations in creative reading & writing (one fiction source, 20th/21st c.) | 1 | 4 | AO1 | Multiple choice (from 2026) |
| | 2 | 8 | AO2 | Language in given lines |
| | 3 | 8 | AO2 | One structural effect across the source (from 2026) |
| | 4 | 20 | AO4 | Evaluate a statement |
| | 5 | 40 | AO5 24 + AO6 16 | Description (image as inspiration) **or** story opening |
| **2** Writers' viewpoints & perspectives (two non-fiction sources, one 19th c.) | 1 | 4 | AO1 | Four true statements |
| | 2 | 8 | AO1 | Summary / synthesis of differences |
| | 3 | 12 | AO2 | Language in one source |
| | 4 | 16 | AO3 | Compare writers' viewpoints and methods |
| | 5 | 40 | AO5 24 + AO6 16 | Transactional writing |

Assessment objectives: **AO1** identify/interpret, select and synthesise; **AO2** analyse
language and structure with terminology; **AO3** compare writers' ideas and perspectives and how
conveyed; **AO4** evaluate texts critically with textual reference; **AO5** communicate clearly,
effectively, imaginatively, adapting form/tone/register, organise for coherence; **AO6** range of
vocabulary and sentence structures, accurate spelling and punctuation (**20% of the GCSE**).

**Summer 2026 onward** (applies to June 2027): Q1 multiple choice; Q3 focuses on a single
structural effect; Q5 narrative option is a story *opening*, description option treats the image
as inspiration; the mark scheme gains a "typical features" column. Marks, timings and AOs unchanged.
AQA notice: <https://www.aqa.org.uk/news> (search "GCSE English Language updates summer 2026").
Papers from 2017–2025 remain valid practice for Q2, Q4, both Q5s and all of Paper 2.

## How AQA tells examiners to mark (apply this exactly)

Level keywords, identical on every reading ladder: **Level 4 perceptive, detailed** (developed,
insightful, exploratory, subtle, analytical, conceptual) · **Level 3 clear, relevant** (accurate,
complete in itself, well explained, directly focused) · **Level 2 some, attempts** (incomplete,
partially correct, emerging, speculative) · **Level 1 simple, limited** (generalised, repetitive,
paraphrased, obvious).

1. **Annotate the whole response first**, then decide.
2. **Start at the lowest level and climb** until the answer no longer meets the level.
3. **A student reaches a level by meeting one or more of its descriptors**, not all. **It is not
   the number of references but the quality of the comments** that determines the level.
4. **Fine-tune within the level**; strong aspects compensate for partial ones.
5. The "Indicative Standard" column is an exemplification of quality, not a model answer and not
   required content.
6. **Nothing of relevance = 0.**

### Caps and notes the schemes state

- **Q5 AO5:** if the response does not directly address the focus of the task, AO5 is **capped at
  the top of Level 2 — 12 marks**. AO6 is unaffected.
- **P1 Q2 / P2 Q3 (language in given lines):** writing only about lines outside the range, or the
  wrong source, with the correct focus → placed in the appropriate level **at the bottom of that
  level** (P1 sample scheme: "either Level 1 or Level 2"). Correct focus is needed for Level 3+.
- **P2 Q2 (summary) and P2 Q4 (compare):** both sources are needed for **Level 3 or above**.
  One-source answers: Level-1 quality → max **1** (Q2) / **2** (Q4); Level-2 quality → max **3**
  (Q2) / **6** (Q4).
- **P1 Q4 and P2 Q4:** reference to the writer's methods may be *implicit* without naming the
  writer; in Q4 the "I agree/disagree" may be implicit. Credit the quality of what is written.
- **P1 Q1 (2026 format):** four multiple-choice items, one mark each, one answer per item.
- **Q3 (2026 format):** structure means pivotal point, juxtaposition, flashback, shifts in
  focus/mood/tone/perspective, contrast, narrative pace, exposition, foreshadowing, rising action,
  anti-climax, motifs — tracked across the whole source, not language in one place.

## Reading ladders

All reading questions except Q1 are four-level:

**P1 Q2 (8) and P2 Q3 (12) — language, AO2**
| Level | /8 | /12 | Characterised by |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | 7–8 | 10–12 | Perceptive, detailed analysis; sophisticated and accurate terminology; judicious range of textual detail |
| 3 | 5–6 | 7–9 | Clear, relevant explanation of effects; clear terminology; relevant range of detail |
| 2 | 3–4 | 4–6 | Some understanding and comment; some terminology, not always appropriate; some detail |
| 1 | 1–2 | 1–3 | Simple awareness; simple/inaccurate terminology; simple reference |

**P1 Q3 (8) — structure, AO2:** same four levels; the discriminator is whether the response tracks
a *structural* choice (focus, shift, sequence, perspective, openings/endings) rather than language.

**P1 Q4 (20) — evaluate, AO4**
| Level | /20 | Characterised by |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | 16–20 | Perceptive, detailed evaluation; critical, detailed evaluation of effects on reader; shows perceptive understanding of writer's methods; judicious range of detail; develops a convincing response to the statement |
| 3 | 11–15 | Clear, relevant evaluation; clear evaluation of effects; clear understanding of methods; relevant range of detail; clear response to the statement |
| 2 | 6–10 | Some evaluative comment; some comment on effects; some understanding of methods; some detail; some response to the statement |
| 1 | 1–5 | Simple, limited evaluation; simple comment on effect; simple awareness of methods; simple detail; simple response |

**P2 Q2 (8) — summary/synthesis, AO1:** 7–8 perceptive synthesis and interpretation, judicious
evidence from both, perceptive inferences; 5–6 clear synthesis, relevant evidence, clear
inferences; 3–4 some attempt, some evidence, some inference; 1–2 simple, paraphrase, simple
reference. **No credit for language analysis here.**

**P2 Q4 (16) — compare viewpoints, AO3:** 13–16 perceptive, detailed comparison of ideas and
perspectives, perceptive analysis of how methods convey them, judicious detail from both; 9–12
clear, relevant comparison, clear explanation of methods, relevant detail; 5–8 some attempt at
comparison, some comment on methods, some detail; 1–4 simple awareness, simple reference.

**Q1** (both papers, 4 marks): points-marked, one mark per correct item.

## Writing ladders (Q5, both papers)

**AO5 Content and Organisation — 24 marks**

| Level | Marks | Content | Organisation |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 upper | 22–24 | Communication is convincing and compelling; tone, style and register assuredly matched to purpose/audience; extensive and ambitious vocabulary with sustained crafting of linguistic devices | Varied and inventive structural features; writing is compelling, incorporating a range of convincing and complex ideas; fluently linked paragraphs with seamlessly integrated discourse markers |
| 4 lower | 19–21 | Convincing; tone/style/register convincingly matched; extensive vocabulary with conscious crafting | Varied and effective structural features; a range of engaging complex ideas; consistently coherent paragraphs with integrated markers |
| 3 upper | 16–18 | Consistently clear; consistently matched; increasingly sophisticated vocabulary, range of successful devices | Effective structural features; engaging with a range of connected ideas; coherent paragraphs with integrated markers |
| 3 lower | 13–15 | Generally clear; generally matched; vocabulary clearly chosen, some appropriate devices | Usually effective structural features; engaging, linked and relevant ideas; usually coherent paragraphs, range of markers |
| 2 upper | 10–12 | Some sustained attempt to match; conscious vocabulary use, some devices | Some structural features; variety of linked relevant ideas; some paragraphs, some markers |
| 2 lower | 7–9 | Attempts to match; begins to vary vocabulary; some devices | Attempts structural features; some linked ideas; attempts paragraphs |
| 1 upper | 4–6 | Simple communication; simple vocabulary, simple devices | Evidence of simple structural features; one or two relevant ideas, simply linked; random paragraphs |
| 1 lower | 1–3 | Occasional sense; simple vocabulary | Limited structural features; one or two unlinked ideas; no paragraphs |

**"Typical features" column (2026 sample scheme) — what each AO5 level usually looks like.** Not a
checklist. Descriptive at L4: distinctive/original imagery, compelling evocative description,
impressive vocabulary used judiciously, conceptualised ideas, subtle detail, impressive control of
phrasing, fluid sequence, highly successful control of mood, crafted style. L3: effective imagery,
clear description exploring details, precise vocabulary, developed themes, clearly shaped sequence,
clear mood, controlled style. L2: attempts imagery, some development of detail/setting, some range
of vocabulary, some sequence, attempts mood. L1: simple/generalised description, basic vocabulary,
simple sequence. Narrative mirrors this with perspective, character, setting, sequence of events,
temporal/spatial shifts, plot and pace in place of imagery and description — at L4 "distinctive
and original narrative perspective", "convincing characters", "skilful temporal and spatial
shifts", "subtle shifts in mood".

**AO6 Technical Accuracy — 16 marks**

| Level | Marks | Characterised by |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | 13–16 | Sentence demarcation consistently secure and accurate; wide range of punctuation used with a high level of accuracy; full range of sentence forms for effect; Standard English consistently and accurately used with secure grammatical structures; high level of accuracy in spelling incl. ambitious vocabulary; extensive and ambitious vocabulary |
| 3 | 9–12 | Mostly secure and accurate demarcation; range of punctuation, mostly accurate; variety of sentence forms for effect; Standard English mostly accurate; generally accurate spelling incl. complex and irregular words; increasingly sophisticated vocabulary |
| 2 | 5–8 | Mostly secure and sometimes accurate demarcation; some control of a range of punctuation; attempts a variety of sentence forms; some Standard English, some control of agreement; some accurate spelling of more complex words; varied vocabulary |
| 1 | 1–4 | Occasional demarcation; some evidence of conscious punctuation; simple range of sentence forms; occasional Standard English; accurate basic spelling; simple vocabulary |

Mark AO5 and AO6 independently. **AO6 Level 0:** spelling, punctuation etc. sufficiently poor to
prevent understanding or meaning.
